Key Concept

Carbocation has only 6 valence electrons — it is an electron-deficient species. The central carbon is sp² hybridized with a trigonal planar geometry and one empty p orbital perpendicular to the plane. It acts as a Lewis acid / electrophile. Drag the model to see the empty orbital from any angle.

⚗ Complete Theory & Explanation

What are Reactive Intermediates?▾

Reactive intermediates are short-lived, highly unstable species formed during the course of an organic reaction. They are not isolated under normal conditions but exist transiently between reactants and products. The three main types are:

  • Carbocation (R₃C⁺) — carbon with only 6 electrons, positive charge
  • Carbanion (R₃C⁻) — carbon with 8 electrons + lone pair, negative charge
  • Free Radical (R₃C·) — carbon with 7 electrons, unpaired electron, neutral
Reactants → [Intermediate] → Products
Carbocation — Electrophilic Carbon▾
  • Structure: Carbon bonded to 3 groups, sp² hybridized
  • Geometry: Trigonal Planar · Bond angle: 120°
  • Charge: +1 · Only 6 valence electrons
  • Empty orbital: One empty p orbital perpendicular to the plane
  • Nature: Electrophile (electron seeker), Lewis acid
  • Stability: Tertiary (3°) > Secondary (2°) > Primary (1°) > Methyl
  • Stabilization: Hyperconjugation, +I effect, resonance (allylic/benzylic)
  • Reactions: SN1, E1, electrophilic addition (alkenes)
  • Example: (CH₃)₃C⁺ — tert-butyl carbocation (very stable)
R₃C⁺ : 6e⁻ · sp² · trigonal planar
Carbanion — Nucleophilic Carbon▾
  • Structure: Carbon bonded to 3 groups + 1 lone pair, sp³ hybridized
  • Geometry: Pyramidal (like NH₃) · Bond angle: ~107°
  • Charge: −1 · Has 8 valence electrons
  • Lone pair: One lone pair in sp³ orbital
  • Nature: Nucleophile (electron donor), Lewis base
  • Stability: Methyl > Primary > Secondary > Tertiary (opposite of carbocation)
  • Stabilization: −I effect, resonance (allylic), electron-withdrawing groups
  • Reactions: SN2, Grignard reactions, organometallic chemistry
  • Example: CH₃⁻ — methyl carbanion (most stable carbanion)
R₃C⁻ : 8e⁻ · sp³ · pyramidal
Free Radical — Neutral Unpaired Electron▾
  • Structure: Carbon bonded to 3 groups + 1 unpaired electron
  • Geometry: Planar (sp²) or slightly pyramidal — debated
  • Charge: 0 (neutral) · Has 7 valence electrons
  • Unpaired e⁻: One unpaired electron in p or sp³ orbital
  • Nature: Neither electrophile nor nucleophile — highly reactive
  • Stability: Tertiary (3°) > Secondary (2°) > Primary (1°) > Methyl
  • Stabilization: Hyperconjugation, resonance (allylic/benzylic)
  • Reactions: Halogenation (CH₄ + Cl₂ → light), polymerization, combustion
  • Example: CH₃· — methyl radical
R₃C· : 7e⁻ · sp² · planar
Comparison Table — All Three Intermediates▾
  • Carbocation R₃C⁺: 6e⁻, sp², planar, +1 charge, electrophile, 3°>2°>1° stability
  • Carbanion R₃C⁻: 8e⁻, sp³, pyramidal, −1 charge, nucleophile, 1°>2°>3° stability
  • Free Radical R₃C·: 7e⁻, sp²/sp³, planar, neutral, 3°>2°>1° stability
Stability: Carbocation & Radical → 3°>2°>1° | Carbanion → 1°>2°>3°
Reactions Involving Reactive Intermediates▾
  • SN1 reaction — goes through carbocation intermediate (rate = k[substrate])
  • SN2 reaction — goes through carbanion-like transition state (rate = k[substrate][nucleophile])
  • Free Radical Halogenation — CH₄ + Cl₂ → free radical chain mechanism (initiation → propagation → termination)
  • E1 Elimination — involves carbocation intermediate
  • Addition to Alkenes — Markovnikov's rule: carbocation forms at more substituted carbon
  • Grignard Reagents — contain carbanion character (C−Mg bond)
Hyperconjugation & Resonance Stabilization▾

Hyperconjugation stabilizes carbocations and radicals. Adjacent C−H sigma bonds overlap with the empty/half-filled p orbital, delocalizing electron density.

  • More C−H bonds adjacent → more hyperconjugation → more stable
  • Tert-butyl cation: 9 C−H bonds for hyperconjugation → very stable
  • Allylic/Benzylic intermediates are exceptionally stable due to resonance with π system
CH₂=CH−CH₂⁺ ↔ ⁺CH₂−CH=CH₂ (Allylic resonance)

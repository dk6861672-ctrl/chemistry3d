Hybridization Concept

Hybridization is the mixing of atomic orbitals (s, p, d) of similar energies to form a new set of equivalent hybrid orbitals. These orbitals point in directions that minimize electron-pair repulsion (VSEPR), giving molecules their 3D geometry. Drag the model freely in any direction to explore all angles.

⚗ Complete Theory & Explanation

What is Orbital Hybridization?▾

Hybridization is a theoretical concept proposed by Linus Pauling (1931) to explain bonding in molecules that cannot be predicted by simple atomic orbital theory. When atoms form bonds, pure s, p, and d orbitals mix to create new hybrid orbitals of equal energy and shape.

n atomic orbitals → n hybrid orbitals
sp Hybridization — Linear▾
  • Orbitals mixed: 1s + 1p → 2 sp hybrid orbitals
  • Geometry: Linear · Bond angle: 180°
  • Examples: BeCl₂, C₂H₂, CO₂
  • Two remaining p orbitals are perpendicular and can form π bonds.
sp² Hybridization — Trigonal Planar▾
  • Orbitals mixed: 1s + 2p → 3 sp² hybrid orbitals
  • Geometry: Trigonal Planar · Bond angle: 120°
  • Examples: BF₃, C₂H₄, SO₃
  • One unhybridized p orbital perpendicular to the plane forms π bonds.
sp³ Hybridization — Tetrahedral▾
  • Orbitals mixed: 1s + 3p → 4 sp³ hybrid orbitals
  • Geometry: Tetrahedral · Bond angle: 109.5°
  • Examples: CH₄, NH₃, H₂O
  • Four equivalent orbitals point toward the corners of a tetrahedron.
sp³d & sp³d² — Expanded Octets▾
  • sp³d: 5 hybrids → Trigonal Bipyramidal · Example: PCl₅
  • sp³d²: 6 hybrids → Octahedral · Example: SF₆
  • Require d orbitals — only period 3+ elements (P, S, Cl, Xe) can expand octet.
σ and π Bonds from Hybrid Orbitals▾

σ bonds — head-on overlap of hybrid orbitals (allow free rotation).

π bonds — side-on overlap of unhybridised p orbitals (restrict rotation).

Single = 1σ · Double = 1σ+1π · Triple = 1σ+2π
